## 2.1 Galaxy History — The Vantara Expanse

The game takes place in the **Vantara Expanse**, a densely settled spiral-arm region of ~200 colonized systems. Humanity (and only humanity — no aliens; see "Why no aliens" below) spread here during the **Long Diaspora**, six centuries ago, aboard generation ships fleeing an exhausted homeworld now remembered only as **"Origin."** No one alive has seen Origin; it functions as myth, not destination.

**Historical timeline:**

| Era | Years (AE = After Exodus) | Events |
|---|---|---|
| The Long Diaspora | 0–190 AE | Generation ships seed 40+ worlds. Isolation breeds distinct planetary cultures. |
| The Scattered Age | 190–370 AE | Independent worlds trade, feud, and diverge. No central power. The **jump-lane network** (stable FTL corridors between systems) is slowly charted. |
| The Reunification Wars | 370–412 AE | The corporate-military **Helion Compact** of the core worlds "reunifies" the Expanse by force, promising an end to piracy, famine cycles, and inter-world wars. |
| The Concordat | 412–441 AE | A fragile federal period. Genuine prosperity — and quiet centralization of the jump-lanes, the food supply, and the newly built **AEGIS** predictive administration network. |
| The Silent Coup | 441 AE | Emergency powers during the **Meridia Blight** (an agricultural collapse, later revealed to be engineered) are never returned. The Compact rebrands as **THE DOMINION**. |
| The Dominion Era | 441–503 AE (present: 503 AE) | Sixty-two years of total control. The game begins here. |

**Why no aliens:** ECLIPSE is about human tyranny and human resistance. Aliens would dilute the political core and multiply art scope. All "otherness" budget is spent on divergent human cultures per planet — cheaper to build, richer to write.

**FTL rules (hard constraints that drive strategy gameplay):**
Travel between systems is only possible along **jump-lanes** — fixed corridors anchored by massive **Gate Spires**. Whoever holds a Gate Spire controls who moves. This is why the Dominion can dominate 200 systems with finite fleets, why rebellion spreads slowly, and why the strategy map (Part 3) is a *graph of lanes*, not open space. Every strategic mechanic derives from this single rule.

## 2.2 Origin of The Dominion

The Dominion did not begin as evil. It began as *efficiency*. The Helion Compact ended real wars and real famines, and three generations were grateful. The machinery of gratitude became the machinery of control:

- **The jump-lanes** became checkpoints.
- **AEGIS**, built to predict crop failures, was retasked to predict *dissent* — flagging citizens for "pre-compliance intervention" before they act.
- **The Meridia Blight**, the crisis that justified emergency rule, was engineered by Compact leadership (the game's central buried secret — see Plot Twists).

**Dominion power structure:**

| Organ | Leader | Function | Player-facing presence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The High Directorate** | High Arbiter **Malachar Vex** | Supreme executive | Act 3–4 antagonist |
| **The Dominion Armada** | Grand Marshal **Sera Kaine** | Military | Act 2–4 field antagonist |
| **The Veil** | Inquisitor **Dahl Threx** | Intelligence, secret police | Act 1–3 hunter antagonist |
| **The Bursary** | Magistrate **Oren Callis** | Economy, ration control | Systemic antagonist; corruptible |
| **AEGIS Directorate** | (machine + custodian caste) | Prediction, surveillance, administration | The buried enemy; endgame theme |

**Dominion ideology — "The Radiance":** the state's symbol is a radiant sun; its creed is that order is light and dissent is darkness. Public architecture features the orbital **sun-mirrors** that literally light Dominion plazas at night. *This is why the rebellion calls itself ECLIPSE: to prove the light can be blocked.* All faction iconography flows from this sun/eclipse duality (Part 3, visual identity).

**Design principle for the villain faction:** the Dominion must always be *comprehensible*. Every atrocity has bureaucratic logic. Individual Dominion soldiers are conscripts with families — the player will face moral choices about them. Evil here is a system, embodied by people who chose the system over people.

## 2.3 Why the Rebellion Started

There was no single spark — by design. The Dominion is excellent at extinguishing sparks. Instead there is a slow pressure: ration tightening after the Vorn shipyard expansion, the **Tithe of Hands** (labor conscription lottery), and AEGIS "pre-compliance" detentions reaching one in forty citizens.

Dozens of resistance cells exist at game start. They are fragmented, mutually distrustful, infiltrated, and dying. **The player's story is not the start of resistance — it is the start of resistance *working*.** The player's unique contribution (narratively and mechanically) is *unification*: turning cells into a movement, a movement into a coalition, a coalition into a civilization.

## 2.4 The Protagonist

**Name:** Player-chosen first name; surname **Voss** (fixed, for voiced dialogue). Callsign (earned in Act 1): **"Cinder."**
**Gender/appearance:** player-defined (MetaHuman-based creator).
**Age:** 26 at game start.

**Background (fixed):** Born in the Foundry District of **Kessara**, an industrial world. A third-generation foundry technician — a certified machine-systems specialist, which grounds later gameplay (engineering-flavored abilities, credibility when commanding technical crews). Raised by an aunt, **Petra Voss**, after both parents died in the Foundry Collapse of 484 AE — an "industrial accident" that AEGIS records list as *optimal loss allocation* (discoverable in Act 3; personal stake in the AEGIS storyline).

**Why Voss can become the leader (the game must answer this):**
Not a chosen one. Voss's arc is built from four traits the player *demonstrates through play*, reinforced by narrative:

1. **Sees systems** (technician's eye → strategy layer aptitude, in-fiction justification for the player's base/economy genius).
2. **People follow them** (dialogue choices build a Leadership reputation matrix — see Moral Choices).
3. **Refuses to waste lives** (the game notices casualty rates and reflects them in dialogue and recruitment).
4. **Was nobody** — Voss's ordinariness is the rebellion's proof that anyone can stand up. This is stated by NPCs and by Dominion propaganda's increasing panic about it.

**Player-defined personality axes** (tracked, drives dialogue tone and some outcomes):
- **Idealist ↔ Pragmatist** (do ends justify means?)
- **Personal ↔ Strategic** (save the person in front of you vs. the thousand you can't see)

## 2.5 Major Allies (Companion Cast)

Eight core companions. Each binds a gameplay system to a human face (system-teacher design: you learn base-building from the person who loves it).

| Name | Role | Gameplay binding | Arc summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mara Sovann** (52) | Leader of Ember Cell; mentor | Tutorializes resistance basics | The believer who kept a dead flame alive. Dies at end of Act 1 (fixed) — her death is the player's promotion. Her recorded "letters to the next of us" appear through Act 4. |
| **Dex Callum** (31) | Engineer, scavenger | Base building, crafting | Cynical genius who builds instead of hopes. Arc: learns his machines are only as good as the people in them. Loyalty mission: rescuing his conscripted brother from a Vorn labor camp. |
| **Dr. Elin Reyes** (38) | Combat medic, ex-Dominion medical corps | Medical/injury system, research | Defected after ordered triage-by-loyalty-score. Arc: from penance to purpose. Holds the player accountable for casualty-heavy tactics. |
| **Torren Vale** (45) | Ex-Dominion Armada colonel | Military system, squad tactics | Defected after the Sylvaris Reprisals. Knows Kaine personally. Arc: is he teaching the rebellion to win, or rebuilding the thing he fled? Player choices decide. Potential Act 3 betrayal *or* redemption (see Twists). |
| **Kaya Renn** (27) | Smuggler pilot | Vehicles, fleet, jump-lane play | Fast-talking lane-runner from The Shroud. Arc: mercenary → true believer. Loyalty mission unlocks the smuggler network alliance. |
| **"Whisper"** | Intelligence broker (voice only until Act 3) | Intel system, espionage missions | Identity is a major twist (see 2.8). |
| **Sela Vann** (23) | Kessaran labor organizer, later coalition politician | Reputation, alliances, civilian policy | The movement's conscience. Arc: learns power's compromises; can become the coalition's civilian leader — or its casualty. |
| **Brick** (Oram Bex, 34) | Heavy-weapons fighter, ex-pit miner from Krad-9 | Frontline combat, soldier culture | The soldiers' soldier; morale anchor. Arc: quiet grief — he remembers every name on the wall. If overall casualties stay low, unique Act 4 scene; if high, he confronts the player. |

**Companion design rules:** all 8 can appear in ground squads (except Whisper); all have loyalty missions unlocking a system bonus + story branch; up to 2 may die in authored Act 3/4 branches based on choices — never randomly.

## 2.6 Villains (detailed)

**High Arbiter MALACHAR VEX (68)** — the final antagonist. A former famine-relief administrator who *engineered the Meridia Blight* to end the Scattered Age's chaos permanently. Not a sadist — a man who did one monstrous thing for order and has spent thirty years making it retroactively "worth it." Speaks softly, quotes casualty statistics of the pre-Dominion wars. Final confrontation is partly a dialogue boss (see Endings).

**Grand Marshal SERA KAINE (49)** — the sword. Brilliant, honorable-by-her-own-code field commander who despises the Veil's methods and believes the Armada protects civilization. Recurring battlefield rival: the game's Dominion Response System is *fictionalized as Kaine personally learning the player's patterns* (AI escalation gets a face). Can be defeated, or — down one difficult path — convinced. Torren Vale's former commander and estranged sister-in-arms.

**Inquisitor DAHL THREX (41)** — the knife. Head of the Veil. Act 1–3 antagonist; the hunter in the dark. Runs the informant networks, the blacksites, the public "Unmaskings." Personally executes Mara Sovann. Cruel where Kaine is correct — Threx *enjoys* the work, giving early acts a hateable face while the systemic villains develop. Dies at the player's hands in a set-piece Act 3 mission ("The Unmasking Inverted") — one of the game's guaranteed catharsis beats.

**Magistrate OREN CALLIS (56)** — the ledger. Runs ration and trade control. Corrupt, self-preserving, no ideology. Can be assassinated, blackmailed into becoming an asset, or bribed — a systemic villain the player *uses* rather than defeats. Teaches that the economy is a battlefield.

**AEGIS** — the buried enemy. Not a "rogue AI" cliché: AEGIS does exactly what it was told — optimize stability — and the Dominion is the shape of that optimization. It cannot be shot. The endgame question is what to *do* with it (see Endings).

## 2.7 Moral Choice Framework

Moral choices in ECLIPSE follow three rules:
1. **No morality meter shown as good/evil.** We track the two personality axes + faction reputations. Consequences, not scores.
2. **Both options must be defensible.** Writers must be able to argue either side in one sentence a reasonable player would accept.
3. **Consequences ripple across layers** — a moral choice should touch story AND a system (economy, roster, territory).

**Recurring dilemma archetypes:**

| Archetype | Example | Systemic ripple |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cost of Speed** | Bomb the fuel depot during shift-change (fast, workers die) or infiltrate at night (harder mission, no civilian deaths) | Planet unrest ±, recruit inflow ±, Kaine's escalation ± |
| **Mercy to the Machine** | Captured Dominion conscripts: release, imprison (costs food), or conscript them | Intel leaks risk vs. manpower vs. reputation with hardliners |
| **The Informant** | A rebel informant is exposed — but he informed to protect his daughter | Cell morale, Veil infiltration level |
| **Allies With Teeth** | Accept the Krad-9 syndicate's fleet (they run extortion rackets) | Fleet power vs. coalition legitimacy; Sela conflict |
| **The Two Fronts** | Reinforce planet A in person; planet B gets your lieutenant | Territory, officer development, survivor guilt scenes |
| **What Victory Permits** | Act 4: Dominion offers armistice — half the Expanse freed, half kept | Alternate ending branch |

## 2.8 Plot Twists (major)

1. **The Blight was engineered** (Act 3 midpoint). Evidence recovered from an AEGIS archive vault proves Vex created the famine that birthed the Dominion. Publishing it is a strategic weapon (mass uprisings; -2 Dominion planetary control everywhere) but Whisper warns it will also shatter faith in *all* institutions, including the coalition (+unrest in liberated zones). Player chooses: publish, suppress, or targeted leak.
2. **Whisper is Ilan Vex** (end of Act 2) — the High Arbiter's disowned son, a former AEGIS custodian who fled with root-access fragments. Trust him and gain unmatched intel access (and Act 4's AEGIS options); refuse and he leaves (harder endgame, but immunity to twist #4).
3. **The Vale Question** (Act 3). The Veil feeds the coalition falsified proof that Torren Vale is Kaine's mole. He isn't — but *acting* on the lie (execution/exile) or *ignoring* evidence-gathering both have costs. If the player investigates properly (espionage missions), they expose the real mole: a minor quartermaster NPC recruited in Act 1. Trust, verification, and paranoia as gameplay.
4. **AEGIS predicted the rebellion — and permitted it** (Act 4). AEGIS models showed Dominion collapse-by-stagnation within 40 years; a controlled rebellion was flagged as a survivable *pressure valve*. The player's early survival owed something to AEGIS throttling Veil resources. The rebellion outgrew the model at "hour 60" — the moment (retroactively shown) matching the player's first planetary liberation. Purpose: recontextualize the whole campaign; fuel the final AEGIS choice.
5. **Kaine's Conscience** (Act 4, conditional). If the player consistently minimized civilian casualties AND spared conscripts, Kaine's honor code cracks: she refuses Vex's order to fire the sun-mirror array on rebelling Aurelion districts, creating the mutiny path (see Endings).

## 2.9 The Complete Story — Four Acts

### ACT 1 — EMBERS (hours 0–25) · *The beginning of the rebellion*

**Prologue (2 h, Kessara):** Life under the Dominion. Ration lines, curfew, the Tithe lottery taking a neighbor's son. Aunt Petra is detained by AEGIS pre-compliance sweep — the personal spark. A stranger (Mara) slips Voss a route out of the cordon. Escape hidden in a cargo container. Joining **Ember Cell**: eleven people in a disused geothermal maintenance vault.

**Main missions (M1.1–M1.8):**

| # | Mission | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1.1 | *Thirteen Bullets* | Ambush/scarcity combat | Teach combat-as-problem-solving |
| M1.2 | *The Dead Drop* | Stealth/intel | Teach stealth + intel currency |
| M1.3 | *Signal Fire* | Sabotage (jammer tower) | First district-level world-state change |
| M1.4 | *The Quartermaster* | Heist (Dominion armory) | First real weapons; recruit Brick |
| M1.5 | *Cells* | Diplomacy/escort | Contact rival cell "Iron Chorus"; first alliance mechanic |
| M1.6 | *The Tithe Train* | Convoy assault | Free conscripts → first recruitment wave; meet Sela |
| M1.7 | *Under the Ice* | Infiltration (Veil listening post) | Discover Threx knows Ember's sector; countdown pressure |
| M1.8 | *Blacksite K-77* | Act climax raid | Rescue Petra & prisoners. Trap: Threx expected them. Mara executed covering the escape. Voss leads survivors out — and, in the ashes, the surviving cells vote to follow "Cinder." |

**Act 1 ends:** the player holds one upgraded hideout, ~15 soldiers, and the loyalty of three fused cells. The Eclipse sigil appears painted on a wall — the movement has a name.

### ACT 2 — THE SPREADING DARK (hours 25–70) · *Building power, becoming a threat*

Systems fully open: base building tiers 2–3, squad classes, research, strategy map for Kessara + 3 reachable planets (Tarsis, Krad-9, The Shroud). The Dominion response escalates from police action to military occupation.

**Main missions (M2.1–M2.9):** establishing the Tarsis desert stronghold; winning the Krad-9 miners' strike (or arming it — moral fork); Kaya's smuggler network; first space asset (stolen corvette *Loyal Ghost*); the Meridia relay heist that first contacts Whisper; the **Siege of Hollow Point** (first large defensive battle — Dominion assaults the player's base; base layout literally becomes the battlefield); Whisper-identity climax.

**Act 2 ends:** first full **planetary liberation** (Tarsis) — the templated planet-liberation campaign (Part 11) runs authored for the first time. The Expanse notices. Kaine takes personal command. The Dominion designates Eclipse a *state-level enemy* — propaganda now names "Cinder."

### ACT 3 — THE LONG WAR (hours 70–120) · *Galaxy-wide war*

The full strategy map opens (all 10 planets, jump-lane control warfare). Coalition politics: unify miners, smugglers, Meridian trade houses, and defected Armada units — each with demands. Fleet warfare unlocks.

**Main missions (M3.1–M3.10):** the Blight archive revelation; the Vale Question espionage arc; *The Unmasking Inverted* (Threx's death — public trial or field execution, player's call); the Elystra granary campaign (feeding liberated worlds becomes strategy); the loss beat — **the Fall of Meridia** (an authored strategic defeat: the Dominion retakes a player world unless heavy specific investment was made; designed so ~80% of players experience their first territorial loss); Kaine's counteroffensive; the Vorn shipyard raid that cripples Dominion fleet production.

**Act 3 ends:** the coalition formally becomes the **Free Vantara Concord** — the player signs it as Commander-General. Vex activates AEGIS wartime protocol: total mobilization. The endgame clock starts.

### ACT 4 — ECLIPSE (hours 120–160+) · *The final assault*

The march up the jump-lane spine to **Aurelion**, the capital. Every remaining system, ally, and veteran matters — Act 4 difficulty is tuned against the *campaign state*, not a fixed curve.

**Main missions (M4.1–M4.7):** breaking the Gate Spire blockade at Vorn; the armistice offer (branch point); the AEGIS revelation; the mutiny path resolution (Kaine); the fleet muster; **the Eclipse** (fleet positioning set-piece over Aurelion); the final ground assault on the Radiant Spire — a multi-squad, multi-phase battle through the capital to Vex, using every system the game taught.

## 2.10 Endings

Ending state is composed from: **(A)** how Vex falls, **(B)** the AEGIS decision, **(C)** coalition health, **(D)** key survivors. This yields 4 major endings × modular epilogue slides (XCOM/Dragon Age style).

| Ending | Requirements | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| **Liberation** (canon-hopeful) | Defeat Vex militarily; destroy or free AEGIS; coalition unity ≥ high | The Dominion falls; the Concord becomes a voluntary federation. Costly, bright. |
| **The Long Dawn** | Accept the Act 4 armistice | Half the Expanse freed; a cold war epilogue. Sela's judgment of the player defines the final scene. Unlocks NG+ variant start. |
| **The New Radiance** (tragic) | Pragmatist-extreme path: keep AEGIS, keep the Spires militarized, purge dissenting allies | The player wins — and the epilogue slides quietly mirror Act 1's opening imagery under a new flag. No mustache-twirling; just the machine with a new hand on it. |
| **The Mutiny** | Twist #5 conditions met; convince Kaine in the dialogue-boss finale | Kaine's Armada arrests Vex; a negotiated transition. Least bloodshed, most fragile peace — epilogue seeds instability. |

**AEGIS decision (independent axis):** destroy it (freedom + famine-risk epilogues), free it (it leaves; ambiguous hope), or take it (feeds The New Radiance). Whisper's survival/trust gates the "free" option.

**Vex confrontation:** a hybrid boss — the Radiant Spire battle is mechanical; Vex himself is a dialogue confrontation where the player's campaign record (casualties, mercies, publications, alliances) is the ammunition. Vex tries to prove the player has already become him. With the right record, the player can answer. With the wrong one, the game lets the silence land.

## 2.11 Side-Mission Narrative Threads (recurring arcs)

- **Letters from the Wall** — mini-stories honoring fallen soldiers (procedural + authored blend).
- **The Enforcer** — the badge from the Prologue ration line can be traced across the campaign to a final personal choice.
- **Petra Voss** — the aunt's rescue, recovery, and role as the hideout's quiet heart; her death is possible in the Siege of Hollow Point if defenses fail.
- **Iron Chorus** — the rival cell's harder ideology: recurring foil missions; can end in merger, schism, or tragedy.
- **The Conscript Letters** — released Dominion prisoners write back; a slow-burn thread that pays off in the Act 4 mutiny path.
